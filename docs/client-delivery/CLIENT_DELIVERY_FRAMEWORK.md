# 1. Purpose

The VCT Client Delivery Framework defines how Virtual Concept Technologies discovers, scopes, proposes, plans, delivers, validates, hands over, supports, and closes client engagements.

The purpose is to make VCT client delivery:

- professional;
- repeatable;
- transparent;
- traceable;
- secure;
- commercially controlled;
- technically sound;
- measurable; and
- scalable as the company grows.

This framework applies to engagements involving technology development, artificial intelligence, automation, software, cybersecurity, networking, technical services, consulting, and training.

The framework is intentionally lightweight. Documentation should support delivery rather than create unnecessary administrative burden.

---

# 2. Core Principle

Every client engagement should follow the principle:

**Understand the need before defining the solution.**

The engagement should then progress through:

    Discovery
       ↓
    Assessment
       ↓
    Proposal
       ↓
    Agreement / SOW
       ↓
    Project Initiation
       ↓
    Requirements
       ↓
    Design / Delivery Planning
       ↓
    Implementation / Training / Assessment
       ↓
    Testing / Validation
       ↓
    Client Acceptance
       ↓
    Handover
       ↓
    Support / Maintenance
       ↓
    Closure

The exact documents and activities may vary according to the service, project size, risk, contractual requirements, and client needs.

# 5. Client Lifecycle

## 5.1 Discovery and Qualification

The objective is to understand the client's situation before committing to a solution.

VCT should establish, where relevant:

- client organization;
- primary contact;
- business problem;
- desired outcome;
- current environment;
- users and stakeholders;
- technical environment;
- constraints;
- timeline;
- budget expectations where appropriate;
- security considerations;
- regulatory or contractual considerations;
- dependencies;
- known risks.

Primary document:

**VCT Client Discovery Questionnaire**

---

## 5.2 Assessment

Where the engagement requires technical or organizational investigation, VCT should perform an appropriate assessment before recommending implementation.

Potential outputs include:

- current-state assessment;
- technical assessment;
- security assessment;
- requirements assessment;
- training needs assessment;
- feasibility assessment.

Primary document:

**VCT Discovery / Assessment Report**

The assessment should distinguish between:

- confirmed facts;
- client-provided information;
- VCT observations;
- assumptions;
- identified risks;
- recommendations.

---

## 5.3 Proposal

The proposal translates the identified need into a recommended VCT approach.

A proposal may include:

- executive summary;
- client problem;
- objectives;
- proposed solution;
- scope;
- deliverables;
- approach;
- timeline;
- assumptions;
- client responsibilities;
- VCT responsibilities;
- commercial terms;
- validity period;
- exclusions;
- next steps.

The proposal should not create commitments that are inconsistent with the eventual Statement of Work or agreement.

---

## 5.4 Agreement and Statement of Work

Once a client decides to proceed, the engagement should be governed by the appropriate contractual documents.

The Statement of Work should establish, where applicable:

- engagement objectives;
- scope;
- out-of-scope items;
- deliverables;
- requirements;
- milestones;
- schedule;
- responsibilities;
- assumptions;
- dependencies;
- acceptance criteria;
- change management;
- security requirements;
- confidentiality;
- support arrangements;
- commercial terms;
- completion conditions.

The SOW is the primary reference for determining what VCT has agreed to deliver.

- scope baseline;
- schedule;

**VCT Project Charter**

# 7. Requirements Management

Requirements translate the agreed business need into clear, testable expectations.

Requirements may include:

- business requirements;
- functional requirements;
- non-functional requirements;
- technical requirements;
- security requirements;
- integration requirements;
- data requirements;
- user requirements;
- training requirements;
- operational requirements.

Requirements should be:

- understandable;
- traceable;
- testable where practical;
- aligned with the SOW;
- version controlled.

Where requirements materially change after approval, the change-management process should be used.

Primary document:

**VCT Requirements Specification**

---

# 8. Scope Management

VCT should distinguish clearly between:

**In Scope**

Work VCT has agreed to deliver.

**Out of Scope**

Work not included in the engagement unless subsequently approved.

**Assumptions**

Conditions believed to be true when the engagement was planned.

**Dependencies**

Conditions, people, systems, information, approvals, or services required for delivery.

This distinction should appear in the SOW and, where useful, the project plan.

---

The project plan should be proportional to the size and complexity of the engagement.

It may include:

- work breakdown;
- activities;
- milestones;
- responsibilities;
- dependencies;
- target dates;
- testing activities;
- client review points;
- deployment;
- training;
- handover;
- closure.

Small engagements may use a simplified plan.

Large or high-risk engagements should use more detailed planning.

Primary document:

**VCT Project Plan**

# 10. Communication and Status Reporting

Clients should receive predictable communication appropriate to the engagement.

Status reporting may include:

- completed work;
- current work;
- upcoming work;
- milestones;
- risks;
- issues;
- decisions required;
- changes;
- blockers;
- overall status.

Status should not be used to hide problems.

A significant risk or blocker should be communicated early enough for corrective action.

Primary document:

**VCT Project Status Report**

---

# 11. Risk Management

Project risks should be identified, assessed, monitored, and addressed.

Typical fields include:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Risk ID | Unique identifier |
| Description | What could happen |
| Cause | Why it could happen |
| Impact | Potential consequence |
| Likelihood | Probability |
| Severity | Overall significance |
| Owner | Person responsible |
| Mitigation | Planned response |
| Status | Current state |

Security-related risks should receive particular attention in cybersecurity, AI, software, infrastructure, and data-related engagements.

---

# 12. Issue Management

An issue is different from a risk.

A risk is something that **may happen**.

An issue is something that **has happened or currently exists**.

Issues should be tracked with:

- issue ID;
- description;
- impact;
- owner;
- priority;
- corrective action;
- target resolution;
- status.

---

# 13. Change Management

Material changes to an approved engagement should be controlled.

A change may affect:

- scope;
- requirements;
- deliverables;
- cost;
- schedule;
- architecture;
- security;
- resources;
- acceptance criteria.

The standard process is:

    Change Requested
          ↓
    Change Recorded
          ↓
    Impact Assessment
          ↓
    Client / VCT Review
          ↓
    Approval or Rejection
          ↓
    Baseline Updated
          ↓
    Change Implemented
          ↓
    Change Validated

Primary document:

**VCT Change Request**

Material changes should not be implemented solely on the basis of an informal conversation.

# 15. SECURE Delivery Requirements

SECURE engagements require additional attention to authorization, scope, confidentiality, evidence, and risk.

Depending on the engagement, documentation may include:

- assessment scope;
- rules of engagement;
- target/asset register;
- assessment methodology;
- security requirements;
- findings register;
- risk assessment;
- vulnerability report;
- security assessment report;
- remediation plan;
- remediation validation;
- executive summary.

For security testing, VCT must establish appropriate authorization and scope before intrusive testing begins.

Where applicable, the engagement should explicitly identify:

- authorized systems;
- authorized applications;
- authorized networks;
- authorized accounts;
- testing windows;
- permitted techniques;
- prohibited activities;
- emergency contacts;
- evidence-handling requirements;
- reporting requirements.

# 17. Testing and Validation

Where a solution or technical deliverable is produced, VCT should validate it against agreed requirements.

Validation may include:

- functional testing;
- technical testing;
- security testing;
- integration testing;
- performance testing;
- user acceptance testing;
- training assessment;
- operational validation.

Testing should produce evidence appropriate to the engagement.

---

# 18. Acceptance

Acceptance criteria should be established before final delivery wherever practical.

Acceptance should answer:

> How will the client and VCT determine that the agreed deliverable has been successfully completed?

Acceptance may be based on:

- requirements;
- agreed deliverables;
- test results;
- demonstrations;
- training completion;
- documented review;
- client approval.

Primary document:

**VCT Acceptance / UAT Record**

---

# 19. Handover

Handover should occur after the agreed deliverable has been validated.

Depending on the engagement, handover may include:

- final solution;
- source code where contractually applicable;
- credentials or access information;
- configuration information;
- documentation;
- user guides;
- administrator guides;
- training;
- operational procedures;
- support information;
- known limitations;
- outstanding items.

Sensitive credentials and secrets must not be stored in ordinary project documentation.

---

# 20. Support and Maintenance

Where VCT provides ongoing support, the support arrangement should define:

- services covered;
- support hours;
- response expectations;
- escalation;
- maintenance;
- exclusions;
- client responsibilities;
- security responsibilities;
- communication channels;
- fees;
- renewal;
- termination.

Support terms should be documented separately or incorporated into the applicable agreement/SOW.

---

# 21. Project Closure

A project should not simply stop when the final technical task is completed.

Closure should confirm:

- agreed deliverables completed;
- acceptance obtained;
- handover completed;
- outstanding items documented;
- support arrangements established where applicable;
- final reporting completed;
- commercial closure completed through the appropriate process;
- lessons learned captured.

Primary document:

**VCT Project Closure Report**

# 24. Version Control

Version numbers should communicate the significance of a change.

## Major Version

Use when an approved document undergoes a substantial change.

Example:

    v1.0 → v2.0

## Minor Version

Use for smaller controlled changes.

Example:

    v1.0 → v1.1

Drafts may use:

    Draft v0.1

Final approved documents should normally begin at:

    v1.0

# 27. Client Data and Confidentiality

Client information should be handled according to the applicable agreement and VCT security practices.

Client information should not be:

- unnecessarily copied;
- placed into public repositories;
- included in public documentation;
- exposed in source code;
- embedded in screenshots without authorization;
- stored in insecure locations.

Credentials, API keys, passwords, private keys, tokens, and other secrets must never be committed to the public GitHub repository.

---

# 28. Public GitHub Repository Rule

The VCT public repository may contain:

- application source code;
- public documentation;
- architecture documentation appropriate for public release;
- blank client-delivery templates;
- generic examples;
- development procedures.

It must not contain confidential client information unless there is an explicit and appropriate reason for public disclosure.

Real client project files should be maintained in an appropriately controlled environment.

May additionally require:

- detailed architecture;

- formal UAT;
- detailed handover;
- support transition;

# 30. Definition of Done

A client engagement is not considered complete merely because implementation has stopped.

The engagement is complete when, as applicable:

- agreed scope has been delivered;
- requirements have been addressed;
- testing/validation has passed;
- client acceptance has been obtained;
- handover has been completed;
- outstanding items are documented;
- support arrangements are established;
- required documentation is complete;
- project closure has been performed.

# 31. AI-Assisted Project Management Rule

AI may assist VCT with:

- documentation;
- requirements analysis;
- planning;
- summarization;
- test-case drafting;
- technical research;
- risk identification;
- reporting;
- administrative tasks.

AI must not be treated as an uncontrolled authority for:

- client commitments;
- contractual approval;
- security authorization;
- legal decisions;
- final technical acceptance;
- financial approval.

Human review remains required for material client-facing decisions and deliverables.

AI-generated content must be checked against the actual project evidence before being treated as factual.

# 32. Change-Control Principle

The project repository, approved requirements, SOW, change records, acceptance records, and final deliverables should remain synchronized.

If the agreed scope changes, the relevant project records should change with it.

The principle is:

**No undocumented material change.**

# 37. VCT Delivery Principle

The ultimate purpose of this framework is not documentation.

It is reliable delivery.

VCT should aim to ensure that:

**The client understands what is being delivered.**

**VCT understands what it is responsible for delivering.**

**Requirements can be traced to deliverables.**

**Deliverables can be tested against acceptance criteria.**

**Changes can be controlled.**

**Risks can be identified early.**

**Security is considered throughout the engagement.**

**The client knows when and how delivery is complete.**

**Future VCT personnel can understand what happened without relying on undocumented personal memory.**

---

# 38. Final Operating Rule

Every VCT engagement should answer five questions:

1. **What problem are we solving?**
2. **What exactly have we agreed to deliver?**
3. **How will we know it works or has been successfully delivered?**
4. **Who has accepted the result?**
5. **What happens after delivery?**

If those five questions are clear, the engagement has a strong foundation.

If they are unclear, VCT should clarify them before proceeding.
